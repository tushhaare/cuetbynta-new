Subject selection

How to Choose CUET UG Subjects: Match Test Papers to Your Course

The right CUET subjects depend on the course and university you want—not simply your stream or the subjects you enjoy. Use this checklist before choosing test papers in the application form.

By CUETbyNTA EditorialUpdated 25 October 2026Free student guide

Start with the course requirement, not your stream

One subject combination can be accepted for a programme at one university and not at another. Start with the latest admission bulletin or programme-specific eligibility page for every course on your shortlist. Note the exact CUET papers, Class 12 subject requirements, minimum marks and other conditions.

The NTA’s 2026 bulletin explicitly advised candidates to select subjects based on the eligibility criteria for the desired university programme. It also allowed up to five subjects in that cycle, including languages and the General Aptitude Test. The 2027 number and rules must be checked in the new bulletin.

A five-step paper-selection checklist

  1. Make a programme list. Write down your first-choice and backup courses at the universities you would actually attend.
  2. Check each official mapping. Copy required language, domain and aptitude papers exactly as shown for each programme.
  3. Build a comparison table. Put courses in rows and required papers in columns. This reveals which papers cover several realistic options.
  4. Check the NTA paper list. Confirm the official subject name and code, then compare it with the syllabus you plan to study.
  5. Recheck before final submission. Confirm the application-year bulletin, subject cap, fees and any permitted correction rules.

Common subject-selection mistakes

  • Choosing papers only because they were studied in Class 12.
  • Assuming similar course names have identical eligibility at different universities.
  • Confusing a university’s admission preference with an NTA examination rule.
  • Using a previous-year mapping without checking the current brochure.
  • Adding papers that do not support any course on your shortlist, then under-preparing for the required ones.

Example of a sensible decision process

If you are considering both economics and a social-science degree, compare the current programme tables at each target university. Do not assume one paper is universally required or that one combination qualifies you everywhere. The relevant answer is the official combination for each specific programme and admission year.

Use our subject-wise syllabus directory after confirming your paper choices, and read the latest NTA bulletin. If a programme requirement is unclear, ask the university rather than guessing from a third-party list.
